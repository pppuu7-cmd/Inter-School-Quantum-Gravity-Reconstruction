# ITER114 preregistration — fixed-geodesic curvature `O(G^2)` radial master-distribution basis

Date: 2026-09-15
Gate: `ITER114_FIXED_GEODESIC_CURVATURE_G2_RADIAL_MASTER_DISTRIBUTION_BASIS`

## Motivation frozen before coefficient reduction

ITER113 retained a finite nonlocal F/M/G basis but did not evaluate the coefficient. In the explicit one-loop fixed-geodesic matter calculation, apparently complicated tensor/line-integral contributions collapse before renormalization to `G_0(x,y)^2` times coefficients, and after renormalization to the two distributions `H_0^(1)` and `H_0^(2)` plus contact terms.

A scalar-curvature insertion carries two additional derivatives at leading order. ITER114 asks whether the complete anchored fixed-geodesic `RR` noncontact answer at `O(G^2)` can therefore be reduced, on symmetry/dimensional grounds, to a small radial log-polynomial basis before individual coefficients are known.

- arXiv:2510.11888 for the curvature-derivative order and the master-coordinate `G^2/r^8` separated result.

## Required predicates

A. Record the source definitions of the separated nonlocal distributions generated by the fixed-geodesic one-loop calculation.

B. Establish the maximum logarithmic degree at one-loop in the known fixed-geodesic renormalization template.

C. Use translation/rotation invariance of the flat Euclidean background to show that the final scalar anchored correlator depends only on `l^2` after all tensor/geodesic-direction contractions are complete.

D. Use `[RR]=L^-4` and the overall `G^2=L^4` factor to fix the separated radial dimension to `l^-8`.

E. Determine whether four derivatives acting on the `H^(k)`-type `L^-4` distributions can generate any separated radial functional form beyond `l^-8` times a polynomial in `log(mu^2 l^2)` of bounded degree.

F. Keep arbitrary coefficients/scheme dependence explicit; do not infer them from EDT.

G. Contact distributions remain outside the radial noncontact basis.

H. A full PASS requires a finite radial basis for the complete noncontact answer, not proof that every basis coefficient is nonzero.
